Concerning the effectiveness of digital interventions by socioeconomic status, the results of the meta-analysis show that there is no particularaly significant effect on physical activity in low socioeconomic status groups (SMD = 0.06, 95% CI: -0.08 to 0.20), indicating that interventions did not quite manage to increase physical activity, whatever the design. In terms of higher socioeconomic status groups, there was a small-to-moderate positive effect (SMD = 0.34, 95% CI: 0.22-0.45), meaning digital tools were relatively consistently effective. Forest plots illustrating the pooled standardized mean difference across all studies that were reviewed included in the low and high socioeconomic status meta-analysis by socioeconomic status metrics can be respectively observed in figures 1 and 2 below.

Results were also even across intervention types, study duration, physical activity measurement, country/region, and health status. The most common Behaviour Change Techniques (BCTs) that were used were self-monitoring (81%), goal-setting (76%), and feedback (76%), though no tangible association was found between the number or type of BCTs and intervention success in groups of low socioeconomic status, although groups of high socioeconomic status benefited independently of the complexity of the BCTs. Post-hoc meta-regression of the number of BCTs used by each study showed no statistically significant trend between the amount of BCTs used for either low or high socioeconomic status groups (seen in figure 3).

Most studies displayed a low-to-moderate risk of bias, though there was no evidence of publication bias (Egger's test: *p* > 0.05). Overall, the meta-analysis results potentially confirm that digital physical activity interventions may add to already vast inequalities between communities of varying socioeconomic status, seeing as the interventions only somewhat significantly benefited groups of higher status as opposed to those of lower status. Barriers that can possibly be observed from these results include low eHealth literacy, lack of customized design, and various socioeconomic constraints (e.g., time, costs, neighbourhood walkability).
